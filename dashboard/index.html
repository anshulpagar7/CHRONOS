01

The life of a job.

SUBMITTED

A job arrives with a priority, a resource request, an optional deadline, and a retry budget. The spec is validated at the door — priority ∈ [0,9], resources non-zero — then handed to the scheduler thread through a command queue.

QUEUED

The job enters the ReadySet, an ordered structure scored by the active policy. Scores age in real time so nothing waits forever — and every dispatch that jumps past a job is counted against the starvation threshold.

DISPATCHED

A bounded skip-scan finds the best job that actually fits a worker's free CPU and memory. If a big job keeps getting jumped, a Slurm-style backfill reservation freezes capacity on the largest capable worker until it can run.

RUNNING

The worker heartbeats on a lease. Miss the window and it is declared dead — its jobs are rescued back to the queue and re-dispatched elsewhere. A failed attempt enters RETRY_WAIT with capped exponential backoff and jitter.

COMPLETED

Terminal, with the full transition history retained. Metrics derive from the event stream — queue latency, turnaround, retries — and render straight to Prometheus. Every millisecond accounted for.

02

Built like infrastructure,
not like a demo.

One scheduler thread owns all state. Everything else — workers, the API, metrics, this page — observes from the outside.

Single-threaded actor core

All scheduling state lives on one thread behind a command queue. run_once(now) is the entire iteration — races are structurally impossible, and the whole engine runs deterministically in a loop.

Resource-aware dispatch

Jobs declare CPU and memory; workers publish free capacity. A bounded skip-scan picks the best job that fits instead of head-of-line blocking on one oversized task.

Crash detection & rescue

Heartbeat leases catch dead workers; their in-flight jobs are rescued and re-dispatched. Proven by a chaos test that kills a live worker mid-job — TSan clean.

Backfill reservations

A repeatedly-jumped large job earns frozen capacity on the biggest capable worker — Slurm's answer to starvation, with the skip accounting to trigger it honestly.

Deterministic simulator

The production scheduler on a simulated clock: 10,000 jobs, twelve minutes of simulated time, ~7 seconds of wall time — bit-identical for a given seed.

Zero dependencies

Standard library only — including the JSON parser, the HTTP server, and this page. Four CI presets, -Werror, sanitizers always on.

03

Measured, not claimed.

Four policies, one bit-identical workload: 10,000 jobs at ~88% cluster utilization. The engine reproduces scheduling theory — Priority starves its tail, EDF protects deadlines, Composite takes both crowns.
